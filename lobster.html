Caribbean spiny lobster

Panulirus argus has no large claws. Look for long antennae, a mottled brown-red shell and pale spots on the tail.

Regular seasonAug 6–Mar 31
Daily bag6 per person
Minimum sizeOver 3 in.Carapace, measured in water
RequiredGauge + permitsSaltwater license and lobster permit unless exempt

How to catch them

Gear

  • Tickle stick and hand net
  • Gloves and a lobster gauge
  • Mask, snorkel or scuba equipment
  • Properly displayed dive flag

Where to look

  • Patch reefs, ledges and hard bottom
  • Under coral overhangs and inside holes
  • Atlantic and bayside structure near Marathon
  • Confirm the spot is outside every no-take zone

Technique

  • Measure every lobster while it remains in the water
  • Gently coax it backward into a net
  • Release undersized or egg-bearing lobster immediately
  • Never use a device that punctures, crushes or penetrates the shell

Rules that matter in Marathon

Regular season

August 6 through March 31. Daily and on-water possession limit is six per person.

2026 sport season

July 29–30, 2026. Monroe County remains limited to six per person, and night diving is prohibited during sport season.

Keep them whole

Lobster must be landed whole. Tail separation in state waters, recreational trapping and possession of egg-bearing lobster are prohibited.

Closed areas: Harvest is prohibited at all times in Everglades National Park, Dry Tortugas National Park, Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary no-take areas, the Biscayne Bay/Card Sound Lobster Sanctuary and specified Biscayne National Park coral-protection areas. John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park is also closed to lobster harvest during sport season.

Rules incorporated September 1, 2026 from the FWC July 2026 Florida Saltwater Recreational Fishing Regulations, plus FWC Spiny Lobster Regulations and Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary zones.
