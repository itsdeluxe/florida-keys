🎣 Collection

Usually taken by pole spear or Hawaiian sling while diving. Use a puncture-resistant containment device.

📍 Where to catch

Patch reefs, ledges, artificial reefs and wrecks. Never place bare hands near the dorsal, pelvic or anal spines.

Recommended gear
Collection toolShort pole spear or Hawaiian sling
ContainmentPuncture-resistant lionfish container
ProtectionCut-resistant gloves and long forceps
CleaningShears and a stable cutting surface; control every spine

⚖️ Regulations

Unlimited bag limit. No fishing license is required when harvested by dipnet, pole spear or Hawaiian sling. Additional area and gear restrictions still apply.

Handling: The fillets are edible, but the spines remain venomous after death. See safe-handling guidance.

Identification Is Critical

Retainable Sharks

Eating
★☆☆☆☆
1/5 · release preferred
Fun / Fight
★★★★★
5/5
Difficulty
★★★★★
5/5 · handling + ID

🎣 Bait & technique

Fresh fish chunks or whole bait on a non-stainless inline circle hook. Use tackle heavy enough for a short fight and in-water release.

📍 Where to catch

Channels, reef edges, beaches and offshore current lines. Do not target sharks without safe release tools and positive species identification.

Recommended tackle
Rod & reel7–9 ft heavy; 8000+ spinning or 30–50 conventional
Main line65–100 lb braid
Leader100–300 lb mono plus species-appropriate bite leader
Hook10/0–16/0 non-stainless inline circle

⚖️ Regulations

Retainable sharks share a limit of 1/person or 2/vessel, whichever is less; hook-and-line only. Atlantic sharpnose, bonnethead, blacknose, blacktip, finetooth, smooth dogfish and Florida/Gulf smoothhounds have no state minimum. Blue, porbeagle, bull, nurse, common thresher and spinner require 54 in. fork length. Many similar sharks are prohibited; check the complete list before fishing. Shore-based anglers need the free shark course and permit.

Swordfish

Eating

5/5

🎣 Bait & rigs

Rigged squid, bonito, mackerel or dolphin belly on deep-drop rigs with lights and heavy sinkers.

📍 Where to catch

Very deep Atlantic water along the continental slope. This is specialized offshore fishing best learned with an experienced captain.

Recommended tackle
Rod & reel5½–7 ft bent-butt/heavy rod; 50–80 wide-spool electric or manual reel
Main line65–100 lb braid with substantial capacity
Leader150–300 lb wind-on and rig leader
Hook9/0–12/0 heavy circle hook

⚖️ Regulations

47 in. lower-jaw fork length with head attached, or 25 in. cleithrum-to-keel if head removed; 1/person, maximum 4/private vessel or 15/for-hire vessel. Hook-and-line only. Report landings to NOAA within 24 hours; HMS permit required in federal waters.
